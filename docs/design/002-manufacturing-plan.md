# 002 - Manufacturing plan (v3)

Generated by `cad/make_manufacturing_plan.py` from `models/v3/components/BOM_v3.csv`, so it always matches the CAD. Everything here is a **suggestion** from the geometry: nothing has been priced, sourced or test-printed, and tolerances and strengths are typical values, not measurements. Part IDs are the ones in the BOM and the file names.

## 1. How to decide: print, cut, machine or buy

Print what is cheap to get wrong (covers, brackets, belt clamps). Buy or cut anything structural, long or precise. Print a first article, measure it, and only then order a batch.

| Process | Typical accuracy | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Home FDM print (PETG) | +-0.2 to 0.3 mm; add 0.2 mm clearance on mating faces | weakest between layers (about half the strength across them) |
| Print fine (0.12 mm layers, 100 % infill) | +-0.15 mm; check backlash on a test pair | gears and racks |
| Outsourced nylon print (SLS / MJF, PA12) | +-0.3 mm (about +-0.1 with a good bureau) | even strength in all directions; no supports |
| Laser-cut or waterjet sheet / bar | +-0.1 to 0.2 mm; holes can be cut at final size | brackets, plates, scissor arms |
| CNC machined / turned | +-0.05 mm or better | pins, sockets, pivots, bearing seats |
| Off-the-shelf | per the datasheet | extrusion, motors, bearings, wheels, belts, hardware |

## 2. Printer and part size

Assumed printer: **220 x 220 x 250 mm, PETG** (change `PRINT_BED` in `params_v3.py`; the export fails if a printed part is bigger). Of the 50 part types that are printed (or print-or-buy), **0 do not fit**.

**What the CAD and the sim now do for printing** (done in `generator/assembly_v3.py`, so the MuJoCo model has the same parts):
- **Racks in segments:** every printed rack is cut into pieces of 40 whole teeth (188.5 mm) so the pitch carries across the joints; the earlier 690 / 481 / 318 mm in-slot racks and the 2 m transfer rack could not be printed whole.
- **Tooth phase made exact:** the crossing piece is symmetric (a tooth space at its centre), the gaps are 1.0 mm and the distances are whole pitches, so the comb is continuous through every crossing (it was 2 mm = 42 % of a pitch out for the horizontal rails).
- **Rails supported:** a laser-cut aluminium bracket per crossing (bearing plate, stems to the posts on both sides, a support for each rail end next to the crossing piece), I-shaped supports where the last stretch passes a post, and a rail bridge half-way up the vertical rail between the two crossings.
- **Column:** now an aluminium bar 28 x 24 x 300 instead of a 300 mm printed part.
- **Thin loaded parts thickened:** solenoid brackets 4 -> 6 mm, jack top lugs 4 -> 6 mm and bottom lugs 6 -> 8 mm, jack motor bracket 4 -> 6 mm, lift carriage 2 -> 4 mm.
- **Bores:** the stage-2 pulleys have a 4 mm axle bore (and bigger index holes); the flip-finger collar is 19.2 mm with a square hole for the tube; the finger shaft is 2 mm higher to make room.
- **Export:** each printed part is laid flat on its largest face (good layer direction) and checked against the bed.
- **Still not done:** the screw and bolt holes (to be drawn per part), the fork or slot that holds the stage-2 pulleys in the middle bar, and the flip fingers stay 4 mm (the gap behind the basket is only 8 mm): print them in nylon or on their side and check them first.

## 3. Joints between printed pieces (modular parts)

**Hybrid joints.** Use plastic shapes to align and to take shear, and steel hardware to clamp. Never rely on a plastic-only joint in a load path.

| Joint | Use for | Do not use for |
|---|---|---|
| Dovetail or stepped lap in the plastic | aligning, taking shear and twist | pulling apart (it only locks one way) |
| Steel 4 mm dowel pins | taking shear off the plastic, exact alignment | clamping |
| M3 / M4 bolt + heat-set brass insert or captured nut | clamping, repeated assembly | printed threads (they strip) |
| Plastic snap-fits / LEGO studs | covers, light alignment | anything that carries load (they creep and shear) |

Rules: cut where the bending moment is low; lap length >= 3x the thickness; two bolts + two dowels per joint; orient the layers so the bolts squeeze them (not peel them); PETG creeps under steady load, so keep stress low. Print a **coupon** of every new joint and pull-test it (a luggage scale is enough): require >= 4x the working load.

## 4. Check the strength first (shortlist)

Hand-calculate these, then run FEA on the worst three or four (layer direction matters: use about half the stated strength across layers):
1. thin printed parts: flip fingers (4 mm), jack pivot lugs (4 mm), solenoid brackets (4 mm), lift carriage;
2. the column at the tray bracket (bolt pull-out), if it is printed or split;
3. the transfer beam, its arms and posts (the cart and robot hang from them) and the rail-end arms (cantilever);
4. the lock pin / socket / bushing and the docking bolt: wear and a one-off shock when the cart arrives;
5. the scissor arms and pivots (about 350 N at the lowest angle), the lead nut block, and the gears (tooth bending).

## 5. Covers and fasteners

Printed covers protect the exposed drives and can be switched on / see-through / off in the viewer and the task page (separately for the robot and the docks). Every cover is held by **M3 screws, never by printed threads or snap-fits** (they wear out): the screw goes through a 3.4 mm clearance hole in the cover into an M3 nut in a printed slot or pocket, or (in plywood) a chipboard screw into a 2.5 mm pilot hole.

| Cover | Covers | Fixed to | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|
| front drive | servo 1, step-up belt, cross-shaft end | tray plywood front edge | 2 x M3 x 16 chipboard screws |
| turn-drive cap | pinion motor, lift servo, carriage | two bosses on the mast | 2 x M3 x 12 + 2 nuts + 2 washers |
| jack motor (x3) | NEMA17 + gearhead | enlarged motor bracket | 4 x M3 x 12 + 4 nuts + 4 washers each |

**Not covered yet:** servo 2 and its gear pair (the corner is too tight against the finger shaft), the two lock solenoids, the climb motors and pinions in the hubs, and the scissor itself. They need their own cover design.

**Totals:** 14 x M3 x 12 machine screws, 2 x M3 x 16 chipboard screws, 14 M3 hex nuts, 14 M3 washers - buy about 20 % spares.

That is why the design uses plain nuts in printed pockets, and inserts are only an optional upgrade for parts you take apart often.

## 9. Order of work

1. Build a **one-bay test rig** first (one rack bay, one vertical rail, the hubs, the tray and picker): it proves the printed parts, the fits and the picker before you buy three rows.
2. Print one of each small part; measure with calipers; test-fit the mating parts; only then print batches.
3. Order the long aluminium extrusion, the plates and the racks (or the nylon prints) in one go; they have the longest lead time.
4. Print and pull-test the joint coupons; run the stress shortlist (section 4) before the cart, beam and docks.
5. Replace any part that fails or wears with the next process up (print -> nylon -> machined) and note it here.
